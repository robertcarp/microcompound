 # Settlement Logic

## What this document defines

This document defines the **settlement logic** underlying the MicroCompound approach.

Settlement logic describes the grammar by which buildings, land, infrastructure,
and privacy relate to one another on a parcel. It operates upstream of building
design, zoning categories, and architectural style.

MicroCompound is not a house type.
It is a **coherent way of organizing multiple dwellings on a single lot** so that
they can coexist quietly, privately, and predictably.

---

## The problem this addresses

Most postwar suburban development in the United States is governed by a single,
implicit settlement logic:

- one household per lot
- one primary structure only
- privacy achieved through distance
- side yards as mandatory separation
- adjacency treated as a conflict risk
- expansion treated as an exception

This logic is enforced through Euclidean zoning, setbacks, and accessory rules.
Over time, it has produced high land consumption, high infrastructure cost per
household, and limited adaptability to changing household structures.

Recent policy responses — particularly backyard ADUs — acknowledge that this
logic has failed to accommodate real housing needs. However, these responses
typically operate as **retrofit exceptions**, not as a replacement settlement
grammar. As a result, they often introduce conflict, awkward geometry, and
infrastructure inefficiency.

MicroCompound proposes a different settlement logic designed **from the start**
for multiple dwellings.

---

## Core assumptions of MicroCompound settlement logic

MicroCompound is based on the following assumptions:

- Multiple dwellings per lot are normal, not exceptional  
- Privacy is achieved through **orientation**, not distance  
- Courtyards replace side yards as primary outdoor space  
- Buildings collaborate to form outdoor rooms  
- Utilities, drainage, and mechanical systems are planned as a system  
- Change over time (rental, multigenerational use, caregiving) is expected  

These assumptions are not ideological. They are practical responses to land
scarcity, infrastructure cost, and contemporary household realities.

---

## Courtyard as the primary spatial organizer

In MicroCompound, the courtyard is the primary organizing element.

Instead of pushing buildings apart to create residual side yards, buildings are
oriented inward toward private courtyards. This achieves:

- acoustic separation
- visual privacy
- predictable outdoor use
- reduced neighbor conflict
- efficient land utilization

Courtyards function as outdoor rooms rather than leftover space.

---

## Orientation over separation

Conventional zoning relies on fixed separation distances to manage privacy,
noise, and fire risk. MicroCompound relies on **controlled orientation** instead.

Key principles include:

- one long opaque elevation where close placement is desired
- openings oriented toward private courtyards
- avoidance of side-yard window-to-window conflicts
- deliberate placement of mechanical systems away from living spaces

This allows close placement **without** loss of privacy or habitability.

---

## Multiple structures as original equipment

MicroCompound treats multiple dwellings as **original equipment**, not as
accessory afterthoughts.

This means:

- structures are designed together
- utilities can be stubbed and sequenced intentionally
- drainage and grading are resolved up front
- HVAC strategies can be coordinated
- parking and service access are planned, not improvised

This contrasts with retrofit ADU models, where secondary units are inserted into
a geometry that was never designed to accommodate them.

---

## Infrastructure as a system, not a patch

MicroCompound settlement logic assumes that infrastructure should be planned at
the lot level as a coordinated system.

This includes:

- utility trenching paths
- electrical and mechanical zoning
- drainage flows
- service access
- future expansion allowances

By making these relationships explicit, the system reduces uncertainty for
builders, inspectors, and occupants alike.

---

## Compatibility with manufactured housing

Manufactured housing is treated as **standard equipment** within MicroCompound.

Its fixed dimensions, transport constraints, and construction characteristics
are not viewed as limitations, but as stabilizing inputs that enable:

- repeatable geometry
- predictable costs
- simplified inspection
- clear fire and privacy interfaces

Settlement logic is designed around these realities rather than abstract
architectural freedom.

---

## Open source as the delivery mechanism

This settlement logic is published as an **open-source specification**.

Open source is used here not to democratize authorship, but to:

- make assumptions explicit
- invite inspection
- document implementation experience
- allow local adaptation without fragmentation
- enable market-mediated adoption rather than regulatory mandate

The core logic is maintained for coherence. Variants and experiments are expected
to emerge at the implementation layer.

---

## What this document is not

This document is not:

- a zoning proposal
- a political argument
- a building code substitute
- a single approved design

It is a **grammar** — a way of thinking about how dwellings coexist on land.

---

## Status

This document defines the initial settlement logic for MicroCompound.
It is expected to evolve through documented implementation experience,
